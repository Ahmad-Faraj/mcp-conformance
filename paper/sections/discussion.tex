\paragraph{Non-normative language delegates the decision to SDK authors.}
The divergence we measure is not, strictly, a violation: the specification never says
\textsf{MUST}. It categorizes unknown-tool calls as protocol errors in prose and
shows one example. That editorial choice left the official SDKs to pick an
interpretation, and the interpretation they picked is now what the ecosystem does.
The written categorization is descriptively false for the large majority of deployed
servers. Where a specification wants uniform behavior from implementations it does
not control, prose and examples are not enough; the requirement has to be normative.

\paragraph{The SDK is the unit of change.}
Attributing servers to their SDK collapses thousands of independent authorship
decisions into a handful of library defaults, and the residual category shows the
same defaults propagating a second time through third-party wrappers. A suboptimal
default therefore reaches everyone, but so would a fix: changing a few SDKs, or
clarifying the specification and updating those SDKs, would move conformance for the
whole population at once. Ecosystem-scale conformance is best treated as a property
of the SDKs rather than of the servers.

\paragraph{Startup failure is invisible to the registry.}
Two in five installable servers never serve the protocol, and a fifth of PyPI servers
cannot even be launched from registry metadata alone. Registries rank servers by
popularity and recency; none surfaces whether a server starts. Server selection is
therefore an empirical reliability question that the available metadata cannot
answer. A pre-publish conformance check---the kind the community has begun to
discuss, and which our harness implements---would turn a one-time measurement into a
standing signal.

\paragraph{Recommendations.}
Two of our findings support concrete action. For SDK authors and the specification:
resolve the unknown-tool ambiguity, either by making the protocol-error
categorization normative and changing the SDK defaults to match, or by explicitly
blessing both mechanisms so that clients can stop guessing. For agent frameworks:
treat silent acceptance of ill-typed arguments as a client-side risk, because
\NoTypecheckRate{} of responding servers will execute a tool on input its own
declared schema rejects, and the result is indistinguishable from a correct one.
